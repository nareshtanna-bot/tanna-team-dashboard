## 1. What the LDO says
- **30 ft comes from the plat, not the zoning.** Zoning is R-20, which requires 25 ft at the rear. The recorded subdivision plat sets the rear setback at 30 ft. [E] Under LDO §6.3.1 ("Setbacks Shown on Recorded Plat"), plat setbacks "shall control." [V] https://files.amlegal.com/pdffiles/Cary_pdf/LDO_Ch06.pdf
- **Features allowed in setbacks: §6.3.2(D)(4), Table.** [V] https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/cary/latest/cary_nc/0-0-0-69939
  - Uncovered at-grade patios and terraces may project into a required yard or plat building line, but must stay at least 5 ft from the property line.
  - Uncovered porches, stoops, decks and steps may also project. The table says "(Roofs may not encroach into building setbacks)."
  - Beyond §6.3.2(D)(4), no building or structure may sit "in whole or in part within a required setback."
- **How each type is treated:**
  - Uncovered patio or deck: allowed in the setback. [V]
  - Covered or screened porch: has a roof, so it must meet the setback. [V: roof rule]
  - Enclosed or conditioned room: part of the principal building, so it must meet the full 30 ft. [I, strongly supported]
  - Cary's permits FAQ says a plot plan and as-built survey are needed whenever the footprint changes, and an as-built survey is needed if work is within 5 ft of a setback. [V] https://www.carync.gov/services-publications/residential-permits-inspections/faq
- **Existing-footprint argument:** **likely fails on its own.** [I] The terrace is lawful only as an *uncovered at-grade* feature. Once it has a roof and walls it becomes principal-building floor area, and the exemption no longer covers it. The terrace is not a "nonconforming structure," because it conforms as a patio. A Town handout reportedly warns that converting a deck or porch into a sunroom "may not meet the current setbacks for the main dwelling." [E]
- **Nonconformities: §10.3.2(H), "Exception for Setbacks for Detached Dwellings."** [V] https://files.amlegal.com/pdffiles/Cary_pdf/LDO_Ch10.pdf
  - A dwelling with a nonconforming setback is exempt from the nonconformity process if the addition "will not encroach any further into the required setback than the existing structure."
  - This helps **only if the house itself already sits at about 24 ft or closer.** The aerial estimate put the main wall at about 38–42 ft and a one-story section at about 29–33 ft. [E] That makes it unlikely at 24 ft. [I]

## 2. Relief paths
1. **Planning Director minor encroachment, §3.19.3.** [V] https://files.amlegal.com/pdffiles/Cary_pdf/LDO_Ch03.pdf
   - The Director can approve up to **15%** of the required setback, which is **4.5 ft of 30 ft**. Conditions:
     - (A) no more than 2 setbacks affected;
     - (B) a construction error, or special circumstances such as narrowness, topography or lot shape that are not common to similar properties;
     - (C) no danger to public health or safety.
   - A separate clause lets the Director grant *additional* encroachments for **structures built before May 13, 1999** that had approved permits, if (B) is met. [V] The house was built in 1992. [E] Whether that clause covers a *new* addition is doubtful. [I] Ask.
   - **At 24 ft (6 ft, 20%) this exceeds the 15% cap.** It works only if the survey shows about 25.5 ft or more, or the design pulls back to that line. [I]
2. **Variance from the Zoning Board of Adjustment, §3.20.**
   - Requires a pre-application conference (§3.20.3(B)) [V], all four findings under G.S. 160D-705(d), and a 4/5 vote. [E]
   - FY2026 fee: a fee in the hundreds for a single-family lot. [E] Board meets around the first Monday at 6:30 PM; notice is mailed and posted. The Town says scheduling "may take a few months." [E] Sources: https://www.carync.gov/connect-engage/town-departments-offices/planning-and-development-services/faq/variances and https://www.carync.gov/mayor-council/boards-commissions-committees/special-committees/zoning-board-of-adjustment
   - Weak case: wanting a bigger room is a self-created condition, not a hardship peculiar to the property. [I]
3. **Plat amendment or re-recording** to restore R-20's 25 ft. Not researched. It might still leave the room 1 ft short. Ask. [I]
4. **Redesign options.**
   - **Set the wall back to 30 ft**, or to 25.5 ft plus a §3.19.3 request. Leave the outer strip as uncovered at-grade patio, which is allowed. Eaves may project about 18 in. [E]
   - **Screened porch:** still has a roof, so no help. [V roof rule]
   - **Uncovered terrace or pergola with open slats:** allowed, but it is not a four-season room. [I]

## 4. What Odyssey's survey should show
1. The surveyed **rear property line**, with monuments found or set and the bearing and distance (219.23 ft), plus the fence location relative to that line.
2. The **plat setback line at 30 ft** (and 25 ft for reference), labeled with the plat (book number omitted).
3. **All existing structures, dimensioned:** the main rear wall, the one-story rear section, chimney and bay projections, each with its shortest perpendicular distance to the rear line.
4. **Existing terrace edges:** the outline of the slab or pavers, elevation (at grade), and distance to the rear line; the pergola posts, marked as open-roof.
5. **Proposed sunroom outline** overlaid on the terrace: wall line, roof and eave line, with distances to the rear line at both rear corners. If the line isn't parallel, show the worst-case corner.
6. A sunroom wall pulled back to **25.5 ft (the 15% line)** and **30 ft**, so the options can be compared.
7. Side setbacks (15 ft) and distances to the nearest neighboring dwelling, for §10.3.2(H)(1)(b) and §3.19.3(A).
8. Easements and buffers, impervious area, the surveyor's seal and date, and a note that the survey is an as-built or foundation survey.
